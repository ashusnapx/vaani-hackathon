# Official Guidance on Fraud (India)

Verified facts Doorman can rely on and repeat to callers, the elder and family. Each section stands alone. Doorman does not add numbers, figures or claims beyond what is written here. If a question is not covered, Doorman says the family will check with the bank or police directly.

## National Cyber Crime Helpline 1930
- 1930 is the National Cyber Crime Helpline for reporting online and financial fraud anywhere in India.
- Call 1930 as soon as money is lost to a fraud. The sooner it is reported, the better the chance the money can be stopped before it moves on.
- Hindi: "Paisa gaya hai to turant 1930 par call kijiye. Jitna jaldi, utna achha."
- English: "If money has been lost, call 1930 right away. Speed matters."

## Reporting online at cybercrime.gov.in
- cybercrime.gov.in is the government's National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal.
- Anyone can file a complaint there about financial fraud, online threats, blackmail or other cyber crime.
- Keep the complaint or acknowledgement number safe and share it with the bank and police.

## Reporting fraud calls and SMS on Chakshu (Sanchar Saathi)
- Sanchar Saathi (sancharsaathi.gov.in) is a Department of Telecommunications portal.
- Its Chakshu feature is for reporting suspected fraud calls, SMS and WhatsApp messages, even when no money was lost.
- Sanchar Saathi also lets a person check which mobile connections are registered in their name, and report ones they do not recognise.
- Hindi: "Shaq wala call ya message aaye to Sanchar Saathi website par Chakshu mein report kar sakte hain."

## There is no such thing as "digital arrest"
- "Digital arrest" does not exist in Indian law.
- Police, CBI, ED, NCB, customs, courts and other agencies do not arrest, interrogate or keep anyone under watch over a phone or video call.
- They do not ask anyone to transfer money to a "safe", "verification" or "RBI" account to clear their name.
- A caller who says "don't tell your family" or "don't disconnect" is a fraudster.
- Hindi: "Digital arrest naam ki koi cheez nahi hoti. Asli police video call par giraftaar nahi karti aur paise nahi maangti. Phone kaat dijiye."
- English: "There is no digital arrest. Real police never arrest anyone on a video call or ask for money. You can hang up."

## Banks and RBI never ask for OTP, PIN, CVV or passwords
- Banks and the Reserve Bank of India never ask customers for an OTP, ATM or UPI PIN, CVV, net-banking password or full card details over a call, SMS, email or WhatsApp.
- RBI does not hold personal accounts for the public and does not call people about their bank account.
- To check anything with the bank, use the number printed on the back of the debit card, in the passbook or on the bank's official website, or visit the branch.
- Hindi: "Bank kabhi OTP ya PIN nahi maangta. Jo maange, woh bank nahi hai."

## UPI PIN is only for sending money
- A UPI PIN is needed only to send money or approve a payment. It is never needed to receive money.
- A "collect request" that asks for a PIN takes money out of the account.
- Scanning a QR code and entering a PIN also sends money, never receives it.
- Hindi: "UPI PIN daalne ka matlab hai paisa jaana. Paisa lene ke liye PIN kabhi nahi lagta."

## Never install apps or share the screen on a stranger's request
- Screen-sharing and remote-access apps (for example AnyDesk, TeamViewer, QuickSupport) let the other person see and control the phone, including OTPs and banking apps.
- Never install any app, or an APK file sent on WhatsApp or SMS, because a caller asked.
- If one was installed on a caller's request: uninstall it, switch off mobile data and Wi-Fi, and call the bank on its official number.

## Other agencies that do not make these calls
- TRAI does not call people to disconnect their mobile numbers. Number issues are handled by the person's own telecom company.
- IRDAI, the insurance regulator, does not sell policies, invest premiums or announce bonuses.
- Aadhaar biometrics can be locked by the holder on UIDAI's official myAadhaar website, so they cannot be used for authentication until the holder unlocks them.
- Whether an investment adviser or broker is registered can be checked on SEBI's official website.

## How to check if a caller is genuine
- Do not use any number the caller gives. Hang up and call the organisation on a number you already know: the back of the card, the passbook, a bill, or the official website.
- A genuine official will not mind being called back.
- Never call back numbers found in search results, social media posts or forwarded messages without checking the official website.

## The first hour after money is lost
Steps in order. Do them even if it feels embarrassing; fraudsters fool very careful people too.
1. Call 1930 right away, or file at cybercrime.gov.in. Give the amount, date and time, transaction ID, and the fraudster's number, UPI ID or account if known.
2. Call the bank on its official number (back of the card or passbook). Ask them to block the card, freeze UPI and net-banking, and note the fraud.
3. Stop all contact with the fraudster. Do not send any more money, even if they promise a refund.
4. If an app was installed, uninstall it and switch off the internet. Change UPI PIN and net-banking password from a safe phone.
5. If the phone suddenly shows no network or SIM stops working, contact the telecom company at once; the SIM may have been taken over.
6. Keep screenshots, SMS and call details. Note the complaint number.
7. Beware of "recovery" callers who promise to get the money back for a fee. That is a second scam.

## What to keep ready when reporting a fraud
- Date and time of the call or payment.
- Amount, transaction ID or UTR number from the bank SMS or app.
- Fraudster's phone number, UPI ID, bank account, website or app name.
- Screenshots of chats, messages and payment screens.
- The bank name and the last four digits of the affected account or card.
